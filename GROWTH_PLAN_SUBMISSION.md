## SLIDE 1: Understanding the Final-Year Student (The Ground Reality)

### 1. Who Exactly Are We Targeting?
- **Core Demographic:** Final-year B.Tech students (Batch 2025/2026) across **Tier-2 and Tier-3 engineering colleges** (JNTU affiliates, Anna University, VTU, AKTU, RTU, etc.).
- **Branch Mix:** 70% CS/IT (highest placement pressure), 30% Non-CS (ECE, Mech, Civil aiming to pivot into software/AI roles).
- **Current Situation:**
  - Campus hiring from traditional mass recruiters (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant) is down or deferred.
  - 8th-semester major projects are due; students are scrambling for distinctive projects that won't get rejected by external examiners.
  - Low genuine coding confidence: They know basic syntax from syllabus C++/Java, but have never deployed an AI web app or used modern LLM APIs.

### 2. Why Would They Care?
- **Fear of the "Blank Resume":** Every senior and YouTube influencer tells them "AI is taking tech jobs; add AI projects to your resume." Yet their college curriculum teaches 2012 syllabus DBMS and OOPs.
- **ATS Filter Panic:** They are submitting 50+ applications on LinkedIn/Naukri and getting 0 callbacks because their resumes lack modern keywords (LLMs, API integration, cloud deployment).
- **Time Poverty:** They don't have 6 months to learn deep math, PyTorch, or calculus. They need a quick, tangible win.

### 3. What Makes Them Register?
- **The Value Proposition:** *"In 60 minutes, deploy a live, working AI application with zero local environment setup, push it to GitHub, and add 3 verified bullet points to your placement resume."*
- **Psychological Triggers:**
  1. **Zero-Risk Speed:** 60 minutes vs. a 40-hour theoretical course.
  2. **Tangible Proof of Work:** A live URL they can showcase on LinkedIn and their CV.
  3. **Direct Placement Relevance:** Explicitly showing them the exact resume bullets before they even sign up.

### Channel 1: College CR & Placement Rep Micro-Bounty Program (Priority #1)
- **The Insight:** Class Representatives (CRs) and student placement coordinators have 100% attention in unofficial college WhatsApp groups (where professors aren't watching). A message from a CR gets a 90%+ read rate within 2 hours.
- **Action Plan:**
  - Identify and reach out to 15 active CRs / Campus Ambassadors across 15 target colleges via LinkedIn/WhatsApp.
  - Micro-Incentive: Offer a **₹100 Amazon Voucher + NxtWave Campus Leader Certificate** to any CR whose batch registers 25+ verified final-year students.
  - Provide them with a pre-crafted WhatsApp message pack + custom referral tracking code.
- **Projected Result:** 12 active CRs × 26 students = **312 registrations**. Cost: ₹1,200.

### Channel 2: Peer-to-Peer Viral Milestone Referral Loop (Priority #2)
- **The Insight:** Final-year engineering students study and prepare for interviews in tight peer pods of 3 to 5 friends.
- **Action Plan:**
  - Upon registering, every student gets an instant unique referral link with gamified placement milestone unlocks:
    - **1 Invite:** Complete GitHub Project Starter Code & Architecture Diagram.
    - **3 Invites:** 50 ATS-Approved AI Resume Prompts & LinkedIn Headline Guide.
    - **5 Invites:** VIP Pass for a 1-on-1 AI Project Code Review with a Senior Engineer.
  - Top 3 referrers overall receive cash prizes: 1st: ₹500, 2nd: ₹200, 3rd: ₹50.
- **Projected Result:** Viral K-factor of 0.38 = **146 registrations**. Cost: ₹750.

### Channel 3: High-Signal Placement Communities & Subreddits (Priority #3)
- **The Insight:** Communities like `r/Btechtards`, college placement Telegram groups, and Discord tech servers despise low-effort spam, but welcome free, actionable resources.
- **Action Plan:**
  - Value-First "Drop-In" Posts: Share a free, open-source 15-line script: *"How to test your resume against ATS using Gemini API in Python"*.
  - At the end of the post: *"We are doing a live 60-min build of this complete web app this Saturday with NxtWave. 100% free for final years: [Link]"*.
- **Projected Result:** 4 targeted community drops = **80 registrations**. Cost: ₹0.

## SLIDE 5: How I Thought (Decisions, Failures & Iterations)

### 1. What Changed Between First Idea and Final Solution?
- **Initial Idea:** Create a generic landing page and run ₹2,000 worth of hyper-targeted Instagram/Meta reels ads.
- **Pivot:** After modeling unit economics, ₹2,000 at an average edtech CPC of ₹25 would only yield ~80 clicks and ~16 registrations. That is a 97% failure rate.
- **Final Solution:** Shifted 100% of the budget into **human distribution channels**: micro-incentivizing Class Representatives (who hold organic trust) and engineering a viral peer referral loop with zero-marginal-cost academic perks.

### 2. If I Had Another 24 Hours, What Would I Improve?
- **Automated WhatsApp Bot with Typebot/n8n:** Connect the asset to a live WhatsApp Cloud API sandbox so evaluators can receive the actual interactive ticket and Google Calendar invite on their personal phone in real time.
- **Live Branch-Wise College Leaderboard:** Build a real-time college leaderboard widget (*"JNTU has 42 registrations vs. CBIT with 38"*) to spark cross-college rivalry and organic sharing.

### 3. What Did AI Suggest That I Deliberately Rejected and Why?
*(Detailed in AI Learning Notes)*
1. **Rejected: Meta & Instagram Ads Strategy.** AI proposed standard edtech ad campaigns. Rejected because ₹2,000 cannot compete with funded edtech ad bids; ground-level WhatsApp CR networks offer 10x higher trust at a fraction of the cost.
2. **Rejected: LangChain + Vector DB (RAG) Workshop Syllabus.** AI proposed a complex multi-agent RAG pipeline. Rejected because Tier-2/3 final-year students with basic coding knowledge would face massive setup failure and abandon the workshop in 15 minutes. Switched to a lightweight, zero-install Gemini API app deployed on Streamlit Cloud.
3. **Rejected: "Invite Friends to Win an iPad/Laptop" Sweepstakes.** AI suggested expensive lottery giveaways. Rejected because Indian engineering students immediately recognize a fake contest on a free workshop. Replaced with immediate, tangible academic utility: GitHub repo boilerplates and 1-on-1 code reviews.
